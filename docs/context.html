Context-Aware Analysis

The difference: Traditional dictionaries give you every possible meaning. Aletheia gives you the right meaning for what you're reading.

Why Context Matters

Words are shape-shifters. The same word can mean completely different things depending on where it appears. Traditional dictionaries list all possible definitions and leave you to figure out which one applies.

This works fine for simple words. But for words with multiple meanings, technical jargon, or domain-specific usage, you end up scanning through numbered definitions trying to match one to what you're reading.

Aletheia eliminates this guesswork. By analyzing the text surrounding your selection, it delivers the meaning that actually fits your context.

How Aletheia Works

When you select a word or phrase, Aletheia captures three things:

  • Your selection - the word or phrase you highlighted
  • Surrounding text - the paragraph and nearby sentences
  • Page context - metadata about what you're reading

This context bundle is sent to an AI model that understands language the way humans do: in context. Instead of pattern-matching to a static database, it reasons about what the word means here, in this sentence, on this page.

The result is an explanation tailored to your reading, not a generic dictionary entry.

Examples

See the difference context makes:

"Cell" in a biology article

Dictionary
cell
1. a small room, as in a convent or prison. 2. the basic structural unit of all organisms. 3. a device that converts chemical energy into electricity. 4. a small group forming a nucleus of political activity. 5. a geographic area served by a single transmitter...
Aletheia
cell
Context: "The mitochondria within each cell produce ATP..."
The basic structural and functional unit of living organisms. In this context, the author is discussing cellular respiration and energy production within biological cells.

"Interest" in a financial article

Dictionary
interest
1. a feeling of wanting to know about something. 2. the quality of exciting curiosity. 3. money paid for the use of money lent. 4. a stake or involvement in an undertaking. 5. a legal concern, title, or right...
Aletheia
interest
Context: "The Federal Reserve raised interest rates by 0.25%..."
The cost of borrowing money, expressed as a percentage. When the Fed raises rates, borrowing becomes more expensive, which typically slows economic activity and helps control inflation.

"Revolution" in an astronomy article

Dictionary
revolution
1. a forcible overthrow of a government or social order. 2. a dramatic change in ideas or practice. 3. an instance of revolving. 4. the movement of an object in a circular course...
Aletheia
revolution
Context: "Earth completes one revolution around the Sun every 365.25 days..."
A complete orbital path around another body. Here, it refers to Earth's yearly journey around the Sun, distinct from rotation (spinning on its axis).
